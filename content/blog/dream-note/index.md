A few nights ago, I had a dream—though perhaps it was more of an "early morning" reverie, or maybe it wasn't a dream at all. Who knows?

Anyway, even though this piece is titled *Dream Notes*—which suggests I should actually talk about the dream itself—the truth is, the moment the melodious strains of *On Wings of Song* began to play, every detail took flight on those very wings, scattering to who-knows-where. While the specifics escape me, I still vividly recall the sensations from the moment right before waking: the excitement, the racing heart, a touch of trepidation. All of it vanished the instant the alarm rang.

I couldn't believe that all that thrill and anticipation had been wiped out by a symphony, like a car going 120 km/h slamming hard on the brakes. I felt myself falling endlessly into my bed, as if my soul had decoupled from my body. I couldn't accept that a mere piece of music had shattered such a beautiful illusion. I desperately pulled the covers back up, repeatedly swatting at the alarm, trying to sink back in and prolong the magic. But dreams, as you know, cannot be put on pause and resumed.

Left behind was only a profound void. The dream was so exquisite that I resented that damn symphony; I couldn't understand why such a beautiful vision had arrived at such a terrible time. I kept spinning "what ifs" in my head: what if the alarm had been set just a little later? What if I had skipped my early class and gone back to sleep? What if I could have stayed in that dream forever? Gradually, an irrational resistance to dreaming crept in. Perhaps I feared the next one would be a nightmare, or maybe I was just terrified that no future dream could ever bring me that same deep sense of satisfaction.

And so, on a crisp May morning, I completely lost the ability to sleep.

Two years ago, when I first met my ex, I described myself as a "not-so-persistent person." I don't recall my exact phrasing, but heaven knows why I chose the most bizarre option out of a sea of possible adjectives. Maybe it was a desire to be unconventional, a weird defensive posture, or just momentary rambling.

Yet, in a way, I am still a persistent person. I am still clinging to that dream. Into that warm dreamland, I surrendered all my expectations without reservation or defense, until it vanished, taking everything I had entrusted to it.

But then again, maybe I'm not that persistent after all. What exactly was the dream about? I honestly have no interest in finding out. The stirrings it brought me—I'll just pack them away and let them dissipate with the night. Since I've woken up, it's time to return to reality. Dreams are non-renewable, but feelings are. Besides, it's far more fitting to seek out those stirrings in the waking world.

Of course, in case you hadn't realized: I am already awake.

And so, I am recording a dream.
